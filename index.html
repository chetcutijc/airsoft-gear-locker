Airsoft home storage guide

A simple, climate-conscious reference for an SSP5 pistol, extended gas magazines, green gas, BBs, holster, and eye and face protection.

Safety baseline: Keep airsoft equipment inaccessible to children and unauthorized people. Treat the replica as if loaded: remove the magazine, clear BBs, engage the safety when applicable, and follow the SSP5 and gas-canister manufacturers’ manuals and local rules.

Safety baseline

Store secure

Controlled access

Use a locked cabinet, locked hard case, or other secure storage appropriate to your household and local requirements.

Before storage

Clear it first

Remove the magazine, confirm no BB is in the chamber, and keep magazines separate from the pistol when stored.

Green gas canisters

  • Store cans upright in a cool, dry, ventilated location, away from ignition sources, direct sunlight, heat, vehicles, and enclosed high-temperature spaces.
  • Follow the temperature range and all handling warnings printed on the specific canister. Do not puncture, burn, or expose it to temperatures beyond its labeled limit.
  • Keep canisters outside the sealed pistol case and separate from the replica, magazines, and other stored gear.
  • Protect the valve from impacts and avoid leaving a can where it can fall, roll, or be compressed.

Gas magazines — SSP5 extended mags

  • Remove all BBs before storage. Do not leave BBs in magazines, loaders, or the chamber.
  • For conventional gas-magazine seal care, leave a small residual charge, not a full charge and not completely empty. Your magazine manufacturer’s guidance takes priority if it differs.
  • Store upright when practical, with feed lips and valves protected from knocks. Do not stack heavy objects on them.
  • Use only lubricants compatible with airsoft seals and the manufacturer’s instructions—typically 100% silicone oil for O-rings and appropriate airsoft-safe grease where specified. Avoid WD-40 and petroleum oils on gas seals.
  • If a magazine leaks, stop using it until inspected. Replace damaged O-rings or have the magazine serviced rather than repeatedly overfilling or forcing valves.

BB storage

Standard BBs

Keep BBs in a sealed bottle or tub in a cool, dry, dark area with stable temperatures. Prevent dust, oils, and moisture from entering the container.

Bio BBs

Follow the brand’s storage label closely. Keep packs sealed until needed and use opened material sooner; a dry container with desiccant can reduce moisture exposure.

Do not leave BBs in magazines or speedloaders for long-term storage.

Hard pistol case layout

Use a padded, lockable hard case for the unloaded pistol and non-pressurized accessories—not for green gas canisters. Keep pressure-bearing magazines in a separate protective pouch, compartment, or case section where they cannot strike the pistol.

Eye & face protection

  • Store goggles and masks in a separate, clean, dry container or soft protective bag—away from the pistol, magazines, metal edges, oils, solvents, and gas cans.
  • Do not crush foam against lenses or leave it under straps, hard items, or heavy gear. Store face protection with its foam relaxed and uncompressed.
  • Avoid prolonged direct sunlight, high heat, and damp storage. These conditions can speed deterioration of foam, elastic, coatings, and adhesives.
  • Before each game, inspect lenses, frames, straps, foam, and retention hardware. Replace protection that is cracked, deeply scratched, degraded, loose, or no longer meets the protection standard required by your field.

Pegboard setup

Yes—use a pegboard for dry, lightweight, frequently used gear, but not as the primary storage for the pistol, pressurized gas, or protective lenses.

Good pegboard items

Holster, belt, empty speedloader, cleaning rod, microfiber cloths, gloves, tools, and labeled accessory pouches.

Keep elsewhere

Green-gas cans, the SSP5, loaded magazines, and eye protection. Use a locked case/cabinet for the replica and a protected bin for goggles or mask.
